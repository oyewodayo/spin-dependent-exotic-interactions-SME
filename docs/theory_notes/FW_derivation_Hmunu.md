## 1. Overview

The SME coefficient H_μν is a rank-2 antisymmetric tensor coupling: L_H = −(1/2) H_μν ψ̅ σ^μν ψ where σ^μν = (i/2)[γ^μ, γ^ν]. Unlike b_μ, H_μν is CPT-EVEN but Lorentz-odd. It does not change sign under CPT, meaning matter and antimatter couple identically at tree level. Its primary experimental signature is an anisotropy (direction-dependence) rather than a matter—antimatter asymmetry.

H_μν has two distinct sectors: the magnetic-like spatial components H_ij and the electric-like mixed components H_0i. These generate different DM potentials at different orders in the 1/m expansion, as derived below.

  ————————————————————————————————————————————————
  **Key Result**

  H_ij (magnetic-like): H_NR = -ℋ^k σ^k where ℋ^k = (1/2)ε^{ijk} H_{ij} → V₃ (dipole-dipole)

  H_0i (electric-like): H_NR = -(1/m)σ·(p × H_E) → V₇ (spin-velocity)

  CPT-even: same sign for matter and antimatter (no Aα = 1 signature)

  Experimental signature: sidereal variation, NOT matter-antimatter asymmetry

## 2. The SME Lagrangian Term

### 2.1 Covariant Form

The H_μν Lagrangian term is:

$$
L_H = -(1/2) H_μν ψ̅ σ^μν ψ where σ^μν = (i/2)[γ^μ, γ^ν]
$$
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*(2.1)*

The tensor H_μν = -H_νμ is antisymmetric and real. It has 6 independent components: 3 magnetic-like H_ij = (1/2)ε_ijk B^k_H and 3 electric-like H_0i = E^i_H.

### 2.2 CPT Properties

Under CPT: ψ̅σ^μνψ → +ψ̅σ^μνψ (CPT-even bilinear). Therefore L_H → −(1/2)H_μνψ̅σ^μνψ, same sign as the original. H_μν is CPT-even. Under Lorentz transformations: since H_μν is a fixed background tensor, it explicitly breaks Lorentz invariance but not CPT.

Under C alone: ψ̅^cσ^μνψ^c = +ψ̅σ^μνψ (C-even for tensor current). The antiparticle coupling is therefore the SAME sign as the particle: H_NR^antimatter = +H_NR^matter. This means H_μν predicts Aα = 0 in the absence of sensitivity differences.

## 3. FW Transformation of H_μν

### 3.1 Magnetic-like components H_ij

For the spatial antisymmetric components, the relevant combination is γ^0σ^{ij} where σ^{ij} = (i/2)[γ^i, γ^j]. In the Dirac representation:
$$
γ^0σ^{ij} = ε^{ijk}Σ^k = diag(ε^{ijk}σ^k, ε^{ijk}σ^k)
$$
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*(3.1)*

This is an even operator (block-diagonal). In the upper components, contracting with H_ij:
$$
H_{H_ij}^{↑} = -(1/2) H_ij ε^{ijk}σ^k = -ℋ_B^k σ^k
$$
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*(3.2)*
$$
H_NR(H_ij) = -ℋ_B · σ
$$
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*(3.3 —- KEY RESULT)*

This is the standard spin—magnetic field coupling, here with the SME background ℋ_B playing the role of a magnetic field. It generates the dipole—dipole potential V₃ in the two-body interaction via the tensor exchange propagator.

### 3.2 Electric-like components H_0i

The mixed components H_0i contribute through γ^0σ^{0i} = γ^0 × (i/2)[γ^0, γ^i]. In the Dirac representation:
$$
γ^0σ^{0i} = (1/2)γ^0[γ^0,γ^i] = -α^i = off-diagonal
$$
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*(3.4)*

This is an ODD operator — it mixes upper and lower spinor components. At order m⁰ it vanishes; the FW procedure generates a contribution at order m⁻¹:
$$
H_NR(H_0i) = -(1/m) σ · (p × H_E)
$$
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*(3.5)*

where H_E^i = H_0i. This is a spin—orbit-type coupling that generates DM potential V₇.

## 4. Two-Body Potential Matching

The two-body potentials generated by H_μν through boson exchange are:

**V₃ from H_ij**

The magnetic-like H_ij term generates a dipole—dipole potential:
$$
V₃ = g^2/(4π) [σ_1·σ_2 - 3(σ_1·r̂)(σ_2·r̂)] e^{-r/λ}/r
$$
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*(4.1)*

where g is identified with the effective H_ij coupling. Both particles 1 and 2 must carry a spin coupling for V₃ to contribute, consistent with the dipole—dipole form.

**V₇ from H_0i**

The electric-like H_0i term generates a spin—velocity potential:
$$
V₇ = g^2/(4π) σ_1·(v×r̂) e^{-r/λ}/r
$$
&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;*(4.2)*

This is velocity-dependent and requires experimental setups sensitive to relative motion between the test masses.
