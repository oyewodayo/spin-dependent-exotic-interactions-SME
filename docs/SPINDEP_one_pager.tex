\section*{Research Question}
Light bosons predicted in many extensions of the Standard Model (axion-like
particles, dark-sector mediators, Lorentz- and CPT-violating fields) produce
spin-dependent forces between fermions. Hundreds of published bounds, from
NV-centre magnetometry, torsion balances, co-magnetometers and precision
spectroscopy, constrain their couplings. Matter-sector and antimatter-sector
results had not been compared systematically, although that comparison is a
natural test of CPT symmetry. Can it be done with the data that exist?

\section*{Framework}
The Standard Model Extension (SME) describes Lorentz and CPT violation through
fixed background coefficients in the fermion Lagrangian. A canonical
Foldy--Wouthuysen reduction maps these onto the non-relativistic
Dobrescu--Mocioiu potentials $V_1$--$V_{16}$, the basis in which experiments
report their limits. SPINDEP, the pipeline I built, compiles the published
constraint curves, matches each matter dataset to its antimatter counterpart
(for example $e$--$e$ with $e$--$e^+$), interpolates both onto a common
interaction-range grid $\lambda$, and evaluates
\[
  A_\alpha(\lambda)
  = \frac{g^{f}_\alpha(\lambda) - g^{\fbar}_\alpha(\lambda)}
         {g^{f}_\alpha(\lambda) + g^{\fbar}_\alpha(\lambda)} .
\]

\section*{Status and Current Results}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Complete spin-dependent dictionary.} $b_\mu$, $d_{\mu\nu}$,
    $g_{\lambda\mu\nu}$ and $H_{\mu\nu}$ are the only minimal-SME fermion
    coefficients with spin-dependent terms through third order in $1/m$;
    $a_\mu$, $c_{\mu\nu}$ and $e_\mu$ give spin-independent terms only, and
    $f_\mu$ is absent. All four reductions agree with Kosteleck\'y and Lane
    term by term.
  \item \textbf{Matter versus antimatter.} Between CPT-conjugate states, the
    CPT-odd $b_\mu$ and $g_{\lambda\mu\nu}$ change sign and the CPT-even
    $d_{\mu\nu}$ and $H_{\mu\nu}$ do not. Checked symbolically for all 16
    coefficient families.
  \item \textbf{Database.} 283 constraint datasets compiled, 247 analysed;
    only 22 involve an antimatter sector (antiprotonic helium, positronium,
    muonium, $dd\mu^+$).
  \item \textbf{Matched pairs.} 15 matter--antimatter pairs (13
    independent). Mean $|A_\alpha|$ ranges from 0.33 to 1.00, and every pair
    is significant after correcting for correlated grid points
    ($Z = 5.1$ to $51$).
  \item \textbf{What that significance means.} For two one-sided upper limits
    $U_f = s_f\,g_\star$ and $U_{\fbar} = s_{\fbar}\,g_\star$,
    \[
      A_U = \frac{U_f - U_{\fbar}}{U_f + U_{\fbar}}
          = \frac{s_f - s_{\fbar}}{s_f + s_{\fbar}} ,
    \]
    so the coupling cancels and only the sensitivity ratio is left. The data
    agree: each of the four pairs built on the 2025 hydrogen bounds has a twin
    with an older matter-side bound, and in every case the tighter matter
    bound gave the larger asymmetry. The asymmetry measures a sensitivity gap,
    not CPT violation.
  \item \textbf{Where the gap is.} 7 of 12 potentials and 6 of 11
    fermion-sector pairs have no antimatter data. The $e$--$N$, $n$--$N$ and
    $p$--$N$ sectors hold 117 matter-sector datasets and none for antimatter.
\end{itemize}

\section*{What I Would Value Feedback On}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Experimental grounding:} are the dataset pairings faithful to
    the experiments, and are there antimatter results I have missed,
    especially in the nucleon sectors?
  \item \textbf{Signed observables:} which antimatter platforms (BASE, ALPHA,
    positronium, antiprotonic helium) could report signed shifts or
    likelihoods, so that a genuine CPT test replaces the comparison of upper
    limits?
  \item \textbf{Two-body matching:} the dictionary is exact at the level of
    one-body operators. What is the best route to matching explicit mediator
    amplitudes?
\end{itemize}
