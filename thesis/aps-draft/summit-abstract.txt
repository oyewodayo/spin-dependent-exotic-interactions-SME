TITLE
Mapping the Standard Model Extension onto spin-dependent potentials: what
matter-antimatter bounds can and cannot test

ABSTRACT (wrapped for reading)
Laboratory bounds on exotic spin-dependent interactions are reported in the
Dobrescu-Mocioiu potential basis, while Lorentz and CPT violation is
parametrized by the Standard Model Extension (SME). We connect the two by
Foldy-Wouthuysen reduction of $b_\mu$, $d_{\mu\nu}$, $g_{\lambda\mu\nu}$ and
$H_{\mu\nu}$, the complete set of SME fermion coefficients that produce
spin-dependent terms. Between CPT-conjugate states, $b_\mu$ and
$g_{\lambda\mu\nu}$ change sign from matter to antimatter while $d_{\mu\nu}$
and $H_{\mu\nu}$ do not. We define an asymmetry
$A=(g_f-g_{\bar f})/(g_f+g_{\bar f})$ and evaluate it on 247 standardized
constraint datasets. Fifteen matter-antimatter pairs can be compared and all
show significant asymmetry, but $A$ built from one-sided upper bounds tracks
the sensitivity ratio of the two experiments, not CPT status: 2025 hydrogen
bounds, which tighten only the matter side, raised it in every pair they
enter. Only 22 of the 247 datasets involve antimatter, and seven of twelve
potentials have none. A useful antimatter bound is one matched in
sensitivity to its matter counterpart; the $e$-$N$, $n$-$N$ and $p$-$N$
sectors offer the most room.
