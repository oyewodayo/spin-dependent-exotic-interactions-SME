Exotic spin-dependent interactions are constrained by many experiments, but those bounds are reported in the Dobrescu-Mocioiu potential basis while the theory is written in the Standard Model Extension, with no dictionary between them. We derive one by Foldy-Wouthuysen reduction of the SME coefficients b_mu, H_mu-nu and d_mu-nu onto their non-relativistic potentials.

Using this mapping we define a CPT asymmetry parameter A = (g_f - g_fbar)/(g_f + g_fbar) and evaluate it against 247 standardized constraint datasets, every potential assignment validated against the source compilation's records. Fifteen matter-antimatter pairs can be compared; all show significant asymmetry. We show A A built from one-sided upper bounds saturates whenever the two experiments differ in sensitivity, whatever the CPT status. Adding 2025 hydrogen bounds, which tighten only the matter side, drove it to 1.0000; a CPT-even channel gives an asymmetry as large as the CPT-odd ones. These are not evidence of new physics.

This yields a design criterion: an antimatter-sector bound is useful for a CPT test in proportion to how closely it matches its matter-sector counterpart in sensitivity, not how tight it is. Only 19 of 247 datasets involve antimatter; the e-N, n-N and p-N sectors are the highest-value targets.
